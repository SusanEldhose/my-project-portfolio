# Formulation and Study of a Frozen Dessert from Hemp Milk

Research project, B.Tech Food Technology (2020–2024), Verghese Kurien Institute of Dairy and Food Technology (VKIDFT), Thrissur.

* Supervisor/Course Director: Mr. Faisal I, Assistant Professor, Dept. of Food Process Technology, VKIDFT

## Overview
Hemp milk, derived from the seeds of *Cannabis sativa*, is a nutrient-dense, lactose-free, plant-based milk alternative rich in essential fatty acids, complete proteins, vitamin E, and minerals. This project develops and optimizes a frozen dessert (ice cream) using hemp milk as the primary base, addressing the growing demand for sustainable, allergen-friendly, vegan dairy alternatives while overcoming hemp milk's known challenges — hay-like/beany off-flavours and emulsion instability.

## Objectives
* Evaluate the feasibility of hemp milk as a frozen dessert base
* Identify optimal pre-treatment and extraction methods to reduce off-flavours
* Develop and sensory-optimize a formulation with acceptable texture, flavour, and melt stability

## Methodology
1. **Pre-treatment of raw material** – Soaking, germination, and roasting were compared for hemp seeds/hearts; roasting hemp hearts gave the best flavour improvement (reduced hay-like/beany notes, added nutty character)
2. **Hemp Milk Extraction** – Roasted hemp hearts blended with water at a 1:10 ratio, filtered through muslin cloth for a smooth, coarse-particle-free milk
3. **Pasteurization** – 72°C for 15–18 seconds, followed by rapid cooling
4. **Ice Cream Mix Preparation** – Sweeteners, stabilizers (pectin), and emulsifiers (GMS) added to hemp milk with coconut cream as the fat source; mix homogenized, cooled to 4°C, and aged 12–24 hours
5. **Freezing** – Churned in an ice cream maker (~30 min) and hardened at –18°C for 24 hours
6. **Experimental Design** – Eight formulations (A–H) developed, varying sugar/liquid glucose ratio, stabilizer/emulsifier type (stabilizer mix, pectin, GMS) and level, and coconut milk/cream quantity & fat content
7. **Evaluation** – Sensory evaluation (odour, flavour, texture, appearance, phase separation) across all formulations; final hedonic scaling (9-point scale, 20 panelists) comparing the two best formulations (G and H) on appearance, taste, smell, texture, and overall acceptability

## Key Results
* Formulations A and B (using untreated stabilizer mix/pectin with coconut milk) showed persistent hay-like odour, rancid/beany flavour, and poor texture
* Progressive optimization (C → H) reduced beany taste, phase separation, and grey tinge while improving flavour, texture, and melt resistance
* **Sample H** (Sugar 80g, Liquid glucose 20ml, Pectin 0.75g, GMS 1.25g, Coconut cream 125ml @ 23% fat) emerged as the best formulation — smooth, creamy texture, stable scoop, superior melt stability, and improved colour clarity
* In hedonic scaling, Sample H significantly outperformed Sample G across all attributes:
   * **66% higher** texture/mouthfeel score
   * **43.5% higher** appearance/colour score
   * **39.8% higher** taste score
   * **32.5% higher** smell/odour score
   * **31.7% higher** overall acceptability score

## Conclusion
Hemp milk was demonstrated to be a viable, functional base for a lactose-free, vegan frozen dessert. With appropriate pre-treatment (roasting), extraction (1:10 ratio), and formulation optimization (Sample H), hemp milk-based ice cream can achieve sensory quality and consumer acceptance comparable to conventional alternatives, while offering nutritional and environmental benefits over dairy-based desserts.

## Future Applications
* Scale-up studies for commercial production
* Shelf-life and storage stability testing
* Flavour masking and profile optimization (e.g., natural flavourants, fermentation)
* Extension to other hemp-based dairy analogues (yogurt, cheese)
* Nutritional fortification and clean-label formulation
